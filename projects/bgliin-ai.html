BGLIIN.AI

An industrial production-monitoring system designed to turn camera feeds into more useful, visible production information through computer vision and a web dashboard.

PythonYOLOv8OpenCVDjangoAngularGPU inference

The problem

Production teams need a clearer way to observe multiple camera feeds and derive product-counting signals without relying only on manual observation.

The approach

Separate camera capture, inference, and browser streaming responsibilities so that monitoring views and computer-vision processing can operate with fewer unnecessary dependencies on one another.

System design

  • Video ingestion: OpenCV captures RTSP camera streams and manages the latest available frames.
  • Inference: a YOLOv8 model processes frames for product detection/counting workflows.
  • Application layer: Django exposes camera and analytics endpoints while an Angular interface presents the monitoring views.
  • Streaming: MJPEG endpoints deliver browser-viewable camera feeds; capture and inference are treated as distinct workloads.
  • Operational records: production counts can be recorded for hourly review and analysis.

Engineering considerations

Multi-camera systems are sensitive to network quality, camera reconnect behavior, browser connection limits, frame resolution, JPEG size, and inference batch size. The architecture therefore needs explicit handling for capture locks, stale frames, reconnection, and the difference between dashboard display rate and model-processing rate.

What this demonstrates

  • Connecting computer vision inference to a usable full-stack application.
  • Working with RTSP video sources, OpenCV, model inference, and browser streaming.
  • Thinking about operational reliability and resource constraints, not just model predictions.

No throughput or accuracy figures are published here because they should be supported by a reproducible benchmark from the target deployment.
